Q2 expanded — the number the seller does not show

The seller’s headline metric is 94% “cash conversion.” Its own definition (CIM p52 n.6) is EBITDA less maintenance capex, over EBITDA — greenfield and prefab capex ($5.4MM), the retainage and underbilling build on the data-center ramp ($4.4MM), ERP Phase 2 ($1.6MM) and integration one-times are all outside it. Put them back and Year-1 free cash flow at the recommended structure is −$1.4MM — an investment year the revolver carries by design — turning positive in Year 2 and reaching $8.2MM by Year 3. The full 13-row bridge, low/base/high, is at §5. Nothing here is a criticism of the plan; it is a criticism of the metric.

Q3 expanded — why downside value is thin exactly when it is needed

The platform cannot be separated from its customer mix. Four-wall value is a function of the rate cards and bid environments that produce it: at the FY28 trough the same 25 branches that support $270MM on plan support $177MM at a distressed 7.5x on $23.6MM — and a forced process at the trough is exactly when the buyers know it. The watch-list is negative value in a workout (closure costs exceed working-capital recapture); the greenfield book sells at cost or below because a distressed buyer pays for ramp, not plan; the licensing structure adds transfer friction in every state, and the Kessler gap (F4) adds it twice; and the surety steps in front of the equity on every open bonded job. Fifty-seven percent of field heads sit in one metro, so the people risk and the collateral concentration are the same fact.
